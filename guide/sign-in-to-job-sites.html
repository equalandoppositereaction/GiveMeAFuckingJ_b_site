How to Sign In to Job Sites in GMAFJ

Which job sites GMAFJ works with, how to sign in to them inside the app, and why your sign-ins stay on your computer.

GMAFJ applies to jobs through your own accounts. It works in a browser built into the app, in your logged-in sessions, so the first real setup step after your profile is signing in to the job sites you want it to use. This guide covers which sites it works with, what signing in looks like inside the app, how security codes and puzzles fit in, and why your sign-ins never leave your computer.

The job sites GMAFJ works with

GMAFJ applies on LinkedIn (Easy Apply), Indeed, Naukri, Internshala, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, Monster, Foundit and Wellfound, and on company career pages, including those built on Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby. The first nine are job boards, and each of them is a site you sign in to. Company career pages are different: they belong to individual employers, they are reached job by job, and they get their own section further down.

  • LinkedIn, where the app works on Easy Apply postings.
  • Naukri, Internshala and Foundit, the boards to sign into first if you are job hunting in India.
  • Indeed, ZipRecruiter and Glassdoor, which carry much of the volume in the United States.
  • Monster and Wellfound, which complete the board list, Wellfound among the sites used for internships and entry-level roles.
  • Company career pages, including those built on Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby, reached job by job.

You do not need all of them. Sign in to the ones that carry the jobs you actually want, and one is enough to get going; you can add more whenever you like. If you are job hunting in India, that first one will usually be Naukri, with Internshala if you are looking for internships. In the United States, LinkedIn, Indeed, ZipRecruiter and Glassdoor carry most of the volume, and company career pages cover much of the rest.

The sign-in is not a formality. A board you are not signed into cannot be picked for a run at all, and working in your own session is the basis of how the app behaves everywhere: it applies as you, from your computer, at a pace meant to keep your account in good standing. Because the app works inside your account, everything that happens there happens to your account too. Applications appear in the site's own history, and replies reach you the normal way, at the email address and phone number on your profile.

How to sign in, step by step

Signing in happens in Profile on Job Sites, the menu in the sidebar where your job site accounts live. The app opens its own browser at the site's sign-in page and you type the login yourself, exactly as you would in Chrome or Edge. There is no form anywhere in GMAFJ where you hand a job site password to the app, and there is nothing to configure afterwards.

  1. Open Profile on Job Sites in the sidebar.
  2. Choose the site you want to sign in to.
  3. The app opens its own browser at that site's sign-in page.
  4. Sign in yourself, with the email or username and password you normally use there. The app never sees or stores that password.
  5. Complete whatever the site asks for on the way in: a code, a puzzle, a confirmation email. The next section covers these.
  6. That is all. The app watches the page for the site's own signed-in signal and marks the site as available. There is no save button and nothing to tell it.

The app notices sign-ins on its own. If you sign in to a site while simply browsing in the app's browser, it picks that up too; there is nowhere to register a sign-in and nothing extra to press. And a sign-in lasts: closing the app or restarting your computer does not end it, because the session lives in the browser on your machine, the same way a session does in the browser you use every day.

What you will see on the run screen

On the Dashboard, the Apply through job sites card is where you choose which sites a run uses. Under Select the job sites to apply on, the sites you are signed into come first, and the rest sit at the far end of the list, greyed out, because they cannot be picked yet. Hover over a greyed out one and it tells you what to do: log in on Profile on Job Sites. The list keeps the same order everywhere in the app, so it looks the same every time you see it.

One signed-in site is enough for a first run, and during setup the app asks for exactly that much: logging into any one of the sites is enough to continue. Signing in also quietly unlocks a second thing on that screen. From the profile you keep in the app, GMAFJ can fill in your profile on Naukri, Internshala, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, Monster, Foundit and Wellfound, so your work history does not have to be typed into each site by hand.

With the sites picked and Start applying pressed, the sign-ins do their job quietly in the background. What a run looks like from the queue to a submitted application is covered in the guide to applying through job sites.

Two-step verification, one-time passwords and other checks

Because you sign in on the site's own page, in a real browser, everything the site normally does during sign-in happens exactly as it would anywhere else. Nothing about the app changes the site's flow, and nothing about it shortcuts the site's checks. That is deliberate: those checks exist to protect your account, and stepping around them is not something the app does anywhere.

If your account on a site has two-step verification turned on, the site asks for its second step after your password. Get the code the way you usually do, by text, authenticator app or email, and type it into the same page in the app's browser. The app never reads, stores or bypasses any of it; that step is between you and the site, and the page simply waits while you do it, for as long as the site lets it wait. If you do not use two-step verification on a site, signing in is just the usual one step, and the app does not require it either way.

One-time passwords work the same way. Some sites email a code, or a confirmation link, when you sign in from a place they have not seen before. Read it wherever it arrives, on your phone or in your own mail, and enter it when the site asks. Nothing in that loop involves the app; it is the site's ordinary security, running normally.

Occasionally a site shows a puzzle or a check to confirm a human is present. Solve it as you would anywhere else. The app never attempts to solve or bypass these checks, and it holds the same line during runs: if a challenge appears in the middle of one, the app pauses the whole queue and leaves the page to you until it is cleared.

Several boards, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter and Foundit among them, offer a sign in with Google button. That works in the app's browser too, including the popup window Google uses to confirm it is really you. If a site offers more than one way in, any of them is fine; the app only cares that the site ends up showing you as signed in.

One habit makes all of this smoother: have your phone within reach before you start. Codes expire quickly, and some sites retire their sign-in page if the second step drags on. If a sign-in fails or times out, start it again from Profile on Job Sites; trying twice breaks nothing.

Why your sign-ins stay on your computer

The rule the app is built on is a short one: you log in to every service yourself, on that service's own sign-in page, in a real browser view. GMAFJ never sees, asks for or stores a password for any job site or for your email. What keeps you signed in between visits is your session, and your session stays on your computer.

Your sign-ins to job sites and email never reach GMAFJ's servers. There is no second copy of a session on some other machine, no proxy standing in for you, and no hidden browser working alongside the one you see. The app works from your own computer and with your own sign-ins, in an ordinary browser. If you ever want the local traces gone in one move, the app has an Erase all local data action that clears what it kept on your machine, signed-in sessions included.

The only account GMAFJ itself holds anything for is the app account you create with your email address. That account is separate from your job site accounts; it carries your profile so your details survive a reinstall, and the Privacy Policy on this site describes exactly what it holds.

The app also behaves the way a careful person would, which is its own kind of safety. It sends one application at a time, waits between applications at the pace you set, and stops the moment a site pushes back. You set that pace in Settings, and the guide to settings and pacing walks through the choices.

One caveat is worth saying plainly, because the FAQ says it plainly too: some sites restrict automated use in their terms. Read the Terms of Use on this site before you connect an account, and connect the accounts you are comfortable using this way.

When a job site signs you out again

Job sites end sessions for ordinary reasons: time passing, a password change on your side, a security decision on theirs. Sooner or later one of your sites will sign itself out, and it helps to know in advance that this is normal rather than a malfunction.

The app does not assume a stored sign-in still works. It counts a session as good only when the site has actually answered it, so a sign-in that has quietly gone stale is not carried forward as though it were fine.

If a site signs out in the middle of a run, the app pauses the entire queue and shows a card telling you to sign in again. It does not keep pushing applications at a site that has stopped listening, because that is precisely the behaviour that puts accounts at risk. Sign in again the way you did the first time, and the run picks back up.

If a site you picked looks signed out before a run starts, the app tells you and offers to open the sign-in page. It does not refuse to start, because the site may still accept the session after all; treat that warning as information, not a wall.

Either way, the way back is the same door you came in through: Profile on Job Sites. Sign in again, complete whatever the site asks for, and the site becomes available again. Expect to do this occasionally for each site; it takes a minute and needs nothing else from the app. If signing in again does not clear it, or a site keeps signing you out faster than you can keep up, the troubleshooting guide is the place to look.

Employer accounts on company career pages

A job board is one sign-in that covers many employers' postings at once. A company career page is the opposite: one employer's own site, reached job by job. GMAFJ applies on company career pages too, including those built on Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby, and postings on the boards frequently hand over to them. The app follows the posting wherever it applies, and fills the employer's own form when it gets there.

Many career pages take you straight to the application form: no account, no sign-in, just the form, which the app fills like any other. On those, you have nothing extra to do, and the posting never raises the subject of an account at all.

When an employer wants an account

Some employers run a careers portal instead, and the first application there starts with creating an account with that employer. Often the whole gate is a single box asking for your email and a Continue button. When the app reaches one of these, it sets the posting aside for you with the link to the page, rather than skipping the job or starting an account in your name on its own. Opening an account with an employer is your decision to make.

Handling it is short work. Open the link when it suits you, create the account or sign in, and complete that application there. The account is yours on that employer's site from then on, and it removes that wall for the postings that follow. The same logic runs in the other direction too: the app does not create accounts on job boards for you either. The accounts are yours, opened by you, and the app only ever works inside them.

One boundary applies everywhere in the app, employer pages included: GMAFJ never types passwords, government ID numbers, or bank or card details into any field. Employer sign-up pages often ask you to choose a password, and that field stays yours. If a form asks for something in that family, the app stops and hands the question to you instead of filling it. The full set of things the app sets aside for a human, consent questions included, is covered in when a job needs you.

A sign-in checklist

Everything above, compressed. Run through this before your first run and most first-day friction disappears.

  • I know which sites carry the jobs I want: Naukri, Internshala or Foundit in India, LinkedIn, Indeed, ZipRecruiter or Glassdoor in the United States, plus any board I already use.
  • I have signed in to at least one site in Profile on Job Sites, typing the login myself on the site's own page.
  • I completed every second step the sites asked for: codes, confirmation emails, puzzles.
  • The sites I signed into show as available on the Apply through job sites card, not greyed out.
  • I know the way back: Profile on Job Sites is also where I re-sign-in when a site ends a session.
  • I have read the Terms of Use on this site, since some sites restrict automated use.
  • I have set a pace in Settings that I am comfortable with.

That is the whole arrangement. You sign in once per site, on the site's own page, in the app's own browser, and GMAFJ works from there in your sessions, on your computer, at a pace you chose. Everything it does after that, it does as you.
